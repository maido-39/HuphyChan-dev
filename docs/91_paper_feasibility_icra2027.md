### 2a. 갈래별 갭 진술 (문헌 에이전트 원문)

**Angle — Robot morphology/control co-design (RL, meta-RL, evolutionary, trajectory-optimization and differentiable bilevel methods): do they derive LOADS for structural **

- Gap statement: Every block of our pipeline has a precedent the reviewer will know: RL/meta-RL policies as design evaluators (Bjelonic, Belmonte-Baeza, SERL), motion-derived actuator torque feeding GA dimension synthesis with transmission ratios (Vitruvio), discrete motor choice under actuator T-N/friction models (Fadini), multi-scenario constraints (Bravo-Palacios), humanoid link/motor co-design built to hardware (Sartore/ergoCub, Ghansah/ADAM), and even the 'sim torques -> actuator selection -> structural analysis' loop is stated in Disney's RSS 2024 paper. What none of them publish is (i) treating RL rollouts under DR/push as a load MEASUREMENT campaign that yields per-scenario statistics of joint torques and 6-DoF link reaction wrenches with an explicit statistic-to-design-value doctrine (RMS/P99xSF, rejection of clip-contaminated peaks), (ii) propagating those loads into link-level structural FEA with directional envelopes and adversarial re-verification, (iii) closing the loop into parametric CAD (closed-form IK pushed to Fusion, measured back from STEP) and parallel-mechanism hard-stop synthesis, and (iv) a human-gait torque-coverage constraint from public datasets. The honest framing is therefore 'a documented, reproducible integration and design-value doctrine with an ankle case study', not a new co-design algorithm; and because no hardware has walked and no strain-gauge/torque validation exists (Vitruvio and Fadini both validated torque/power on real robots), the claim that RL-measured loads are valid design loads remains unsupported and must be stated as a limitation, not a result.
- Caveats: HAL, ETH Research Collection, IEEE Xplore, ResearchGate, scispace and (on retry) the Semantic Scholar API were blocked or rate-limited, so the Fadini ICRA 2021 and Vitruvio abstracts are reconstructed from search snippets and Crossref metadata (medium confidence) rather than read directly; the Fadini Robotica 2024 companion paper was read and is used as the high-confidence anchor. The Grandia (Disney) and SERL full-text statements come from a summarizer pass over the arXiv HTML, not my own reading of the PDF; the Disney quote should be re-checked against the PDF before citing it verbatim. I did not verify Luck et al.'s exact morphology parameters from the full text. Two further candidates were found but not included for lack of verified abstracts: the MDPI Actuators 2025 'Design of Actuators for a Humanoid Robot with Anthropomorphic Characteristics and Running Capability' (403) and Fadini et al. 'Simulation Aided Co-Design for Robust Robot Optimization' (RA-L 2022); both are worth checking because they sit between actuator sizing and hardware realization.

**Angle — parallel-ankle-and-stops: 2-motor pushrod/RSU/linkage ankle mechanisms for humanoids, dimension synthesis under actuator/joint constraints, and hard-stop / rang**

- Gap statement: Taken together, the literature already contains every individual ingredient of our ankle case study: closed-form IK and closure-feasible parameterization of an RSU ankle with task-driven multi-objective synthesis under actuator-rating and collision constraints (Cervettini 2025), human-mocap inverse-dynamics loads plus torque-speed/thermal actuator envelopes inside a geometry optimization (Kaminaga 2013), passive-joint (ball-joint) angle limits and collision constraints in PKM synthesis (Durgesh/Kumar/Chablat 2022), architecture metrics for Cassie/Digit-style ankles (Batto 2023), and hard stops plus stress/fatigue verification on a linkage leg (Mithra 2025). What we did not find anywhere is (a) load inputs taken as statistics over replayed RL policies under domain randomization and push disturbances with an explicit doctrine separating thermal (RMS), transient (P99) and structural (P99 wrench x SF) design values and documenting peak provenance, (b) a formal argument that per-motor crank stops cannot bound foot pose in a 2-RSU ankle (crank-angle correlation, rectangle-in-crank-space overshoot) and that a gimbal-axis stop is necessary and sufficient, with stop loads re-based on measured residual moments, and (c) a closed loop from optimum to parametric CAD and back-measurement from STEP. A skeptical reviewer will classify (a)-(c) as integration rather than new methods, and will note that Cervettini, Kaminaga, WALK-MAN and Mithra all built and tested hardware while we have none, so the defensible novelty claim is the reproducible end-to-end process and the stop-sufficiency proof, and it needs at least a bench or sim-to-real check of the load statistics to survive review.
- Caveats: 1) 'Kumar 2022 spherical ankle' in the brief could not be matched to a 2022 paper; the candidates are Kumar et al. 2018 ARK (RH5 2SPRR+1U ankle), Kumar, Bongardt, Simnofske, Kirchner 'Active Ankle' almost-spherical 3-DoF mechanism (J. Intell. Robot. Syst. 94, 2019, DOI 10.1007/s10846-018-0792-x, exoskeleton-oriented, abstract seen only via search snippet), and Durgesh et al. 2022 with Kumar as co-author. 2) No peer-reviewed design paper exists for the Unitree G1 or Booster T1 ankle as far as I could find; only vendor docs (G1 'PR/AB' ankle modes) and control-side papers (Lutz 2025; also arXiv 2504.04767 'Extended URDF' and 2507.00273 on RL with parallel actuation, not fetched). 3) Other relevant items found but not fully verified: A. Abate, 'Mechanical Design for Robot Locomotion', PhD thesis, Oregon State Univ. 2018 (Cassie leg design; repository page rendered empty), and Xiao, Zeng, Jia, Xu, Luo, 'Design of a Humanoid Robot Leg with Ankle-Knee Parallel Mechanism', IEEE ROBIO 2024, DOI 10.1109/ROBIO64047.2024.10907561 (2-RSS ankle driven by coaxial knee cranks, 30.3% inertia reduction; OpenAlex abstract read). Kangaroo (Roig et al., ICRA 2022 legged-robots workshop, HAL hal-03669855) and LOLA (Lohmeier et al.) are further linear-actuator parallel-ankle precedents cited by Cervettini but not fetched here. 4) Several publisher pages (IEEE Xplore, ASME, MDPI, Springer, HAL) blocked fetching; metadata for those was confirmed through Crossref/OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar APIs, and confidence is marked accordingly. 5) The Mithra hard-stop/D3O details come from a search snippet of the MDPI page, not the full text.

**Angle — structural-fea-pipelines: multibody/controlled-simulation loads -> FEA / topology optimization of legged-robot links; actuator and mechanism sizing from dynamic**

- Gap statement: Every stage of our chain has a published precedent: controller-in-the-loop multibody loads feeding topology optimization of humanoid parts (Albers and Ottnad 2007/2008, LOLA 2006), scenario-based MBD-to-FEA-to-GA link optimization with a prototype (Geonea et al. 2026), dynamic-trajectory-driven actuator/linkage sizing (Ding 2021, Zhang et al. 2025), RL rollouts driving a hardware parameter with hardware validation (Bjelonic 2023), and task-informed multi-objective RSU ankle synthesis (Cervettini et al. 2025), while Disney (Grandia 2024) and Berkeley Humanoid (Liao 2024) show the informal sim-to-design loop is routine. What I did not find anywhere is a learned policy under domain randomization, terrain and push groups used as a stochastic load generator whose percentile statistics (RMS thermal, P99 x1.25 transient, P99 wrench x SF structural, with explicit rejection of raw peaks on provenance grounds) are then carried as hard constraints through mechanism synthesis, 6-DoF link-wrench envelopes by unit-load superposition with sign enumeration, bolt/thread checks and hard-stop loads, with adversarial re-verification on record. The honest claim is therefore not 'no prior end-to-end process' but 'first to make the load-statistics doctrine from RL rollouts explicit, propagate it consistently across all downstream stages, and publish it reproducibly'; a reviewer will also note that Geonea 2026 and Bjelonic 2023 have physical validation and we have none yet.
- Caveats: Verification limits: Semantic Scholar and IEEE Xplore rate-limited/blocked; Albers 2007/2008 abstracts were reconstructed from OpenAlex inverted indexes (bib data confirmed via Crossref) but full texts were not read, so the exact ARMAR load-case procedure is not verified. LOLA's load-case source for topology optimization is not stated in the 2006 abstract; Lohmeier's 2010 TUM dissertation likely documents it but was not read. Additional papers verified but omitted from the list for space: Bravo-Palacios and Wensing 2022 IROS ADMM co-design with terrain stochastic programming (DOI 10.1109/IROS47612.2022.9981641, no FEA); Sun et al. 2023 Sci Rep topology-optimized compliant quadruped legs (static 5 N / 5 mm load cases); Zong et al. 2024 Bio-Design and Manufacturing hydraulic limb-leg AM+TO with 24.5% mass reduction (load source not in abstract); Zhang, Li, Ying 2025 IJPEM Fusion 360 generative design + ANSYS TO of a biped arch part (loads unspecified); Zhu et al. 2024 ICMD hydraulic biped thigh TO (medium confidence, snippet only); Vanteddu et al. 2025 arXiv 2509.14935 CAD-driven co-design of jet humanoids (5000 CAD designs, MPC evaluation, no FEA); Liao et al. 2024 Berkeley Humanoid (durability by recorded fall counts, no FEA described). I found no paper that uses RL rollouts specifically as FEA load input; this is absence of evidence from English web search only (no Scopus/IEEE full-text search), so a reviewer may still know one. Bolt/fastener verification in robot structures returned only generic VDI 2230 / NASA guideline material, no robot-specific paper. Search date 2026-08-23.

**Angle — humanoid-hw-papers: published humanoid/biped (and reference quadruped) hardware-design papers and how they sized actuators and structure; specifically which use**

- Gap statement: Every link of our chain has a published precedent a reviewer will know: controller-simulation torque demand used to pick a transmission ratio (HECTOR), multibody-gait loads feeding topology optimization/FEA of humanoid links in a closed loop with the controller (Albers & Ottnad 2008; Lola 2006-2009), RSU parallel-ankle geometry synthesized against walking trajectories from experiment and simulation with closed-form IK (Cervettini et al., Humanoids 2025), RL-in-the-loop dimensional design of a biped (SERL, IROS 2024) and hardware-validated RL co-design (Bjelonic 2023), FEA/fatigue verification against running loads (Mithra 2025), and human-biomechanics actuator envelopes (Sunbeam 2025). What I could not find in any of these is (a) RL-policy replay across terrain/DR/push groups used as the load generator with an explicit statistical design-value doctrine (RMS for thermal, P99 x factor for transient/structural, raw peaks excluded with provenance audit), (b) deriving hard-stop/safety architecture for a parallel ankle from the same load statistics and mechanism kinematics, and (c) a documented closed-form-to-parametric-CAD round trip with adversarial red-team reversals. Those are integration and process contributions, not new methods; the paper must be framed as an open, reproducible process study positioned as an increment on Cervettini/Albers/Lola, and its weakest point is that all the cited precedents except Sunbeam built and ran hardware while ours has not walked.
- Caveats: Coverage limits: (1) TRON1 (LimX), Unitree H1/G1 and Booster have only product pages/spec sheets, no peer-reviewed hardware-design paper found; Cassie/Digit have no Agility design paper, so the Abate thesis is the substitute. (4) Hosts unreachable from this machine: TUM mediaTUM and KIT (ECONNREFUSED), MDPI (403), IEEE Xplore and Springer (blank/redirect); entries marked medium rely on Crossref metadata, the TUM portal abstract, or search snippets. ### 3b. 주요 약점(발췌)
- 설계값 계수(×1.25, ×1.5, ×1.875, ×2)는 관례이지 결과가 아님 — 고장모드·듀티·피로·정량 불확실성에서 유도되지 않음.
- 인간 보행 커버리지: 질량만으로 스케일(다리길이·속도 제외), 로봇 각도 사용이 인간과 "반전"([75 §2](75_human_ankle_alignment.md))인데 토크 커버리지를 하드제약으로 — 정당화 필요 또는 목적함수로.
- 크랭크 스톱 논증은 "2-DoF 결합 병렬기구라면 당연"한 수준; §15에서 28° 스윙 허용 시 문제가 해소되므로 기하-조건부로 서술.
- 최적해가 3-제약 코너, 마진 3.4–4.1 %, A_L 감도 −0.4 pp/0.1 mm, as-built 편차 0.2–1.7 mm — **공차 강건성 없음**.
- 멀티에이전트/레드팀 방법론([42](42_research_methodology.md))은 로보틱스 기여가 아님 → 검증 프로토콜 한 문장 + 보충자료.
- 재현성: FEA 산출물이 리포 밖(`~/pyg_fea/work`), 메시 git 제외, 데이터 릴리스·태그 없음, 8/13 이후 164 커밋 중 교정 커밋 18개.
- 무릎이 안 닫힘(열 112–126 %), rough 정책이 명령 41–57 %만 추종 — "왜 발목 수치는 믿나"를 유발.

## 부록 A. 리뷰어 원문 (StructuredOutput 그대로)

### ICRA Reviewer 1 — score 2/5

**Recommendation.** Reject for ICRA 2027 main track as an 'end-to-end process for which no prior work exists' paper; the chain is known (Albers & Ottnad 2007/2008 controller-in-the-loop loads -> topology optimization; Lola 2006; Vitruvio 2020 motion->torque->dimension synthesis with hardware torque check; Geonea 2026 MBD->FEA->GA->prototype; Cervettini 2025 RSU ankle synthesis with closed-form IK). The gap is partly a publication-culture artifact (system papers compress the process to a paragraph) and partly real but narrow: nobody has formalized how to turn STOCHASTIC learned-policy rollouts (DR/terrain/push) into design values, nor documented the censoring of peaks by actuator clips, nor given the crank-stop insufficiency argument for a 2-RSU ankle. Those narrow pieces are publishable, but only with a validation that the doctrine's numbers mean something: right now the authors' own tables show design values move 2-3x between policy generations (ankle_pitch P99 23->55->60 N·m, knee P99 42->114 N·m) while the formalized bootstrap CIs are ±2-7% — the doctrine quantifies the wrong variance. Do not submit in 23 days. Re-target IEEE RA-L (rolling; IROS 2027 transfer window open to 30 Apr 2027, so submit by ~Dec 2026) after: (1) the freeze retrain on the final v3 model and re-measurement, (2) a sim-to-sim closed-chain re-measurement of the ankle loads (the loop-ankle MJCF already exists), (3) a cross-policy/cross-seed envelope table, and (4) a single-ankle bench test of the J^T/IK load chain. Optionally send the stop-sufficiency geometry as an ICRA 2027 workshop/LBR note. If the team insists on ICRA 2027, narrow to 'design-load doctrine + ankle synthesis + sim-to-sim validation', cut FEA to one paragraph, and drop every 'first end-to-end' sentence.

**Fatal (would reject):**
- The central claim — that statistics of RL-policy rollouts under DR/push are valid hardware design loads — is unsupported by any validation (no bench, no strain gauge, no torque/current check, no sim-to-real), and the authors' own data undermine it: between policy generations the 'measured' P99 moved 2.6x at ankle_pitch (23 -> 55 -> 60 N·m) and 2.7x at knee (42 -> 114 N·m), i.e. the loads are a property of the reward/curriculum, not of the robot. A ±2-7% bootstrap CI on a quantity that shifts 200% with the designer's reward choice is not a design-value doctrine; the paper formalizes sampling variance while the dominant uncertainty (policy class) is unaddressed. Vitruvio and Fadini validated torque on hardware; Bjelonic 2023, Geonea 2026, Cervettini 2025, Kaminaga 2013 all built hardware. A reviewer will not accept 'limitation' wording as a substitute for the paper's only load-bearing claim.
- Circular / censored load measurement for the ankle case study: the policies were trained and replayed on a SERIAL pitch/roll hinge model with the OLD actuator caps (RS03 pitch ±60, RS00 roll ±14-20), then mapped to the 2-RSU by J^T post hoc. The 2-RSU (2x RS03 co-driven) has a different capacity, different coupling and a pose-dependent Jacobian; the demand pool that constrains its synthesis (motor P99 x1.25 <= 60, T-N envelope, rod force) was shaped by actuator limits the design does not have, and P99 sits near the clip (knee 112 vs 120 clip, 0.69% occupancy). Lutz et al. 2025 show serial-proxy vs true-transmission policies differ; the repo already has a closed-chain loop-ankle MJCF (robot model v2/v21) and did not use it for measurement. Designing a mechanism from demand censored by a different mechanism is a methodological hole, not a limitation.
- Every downstream number is computed from a stale model: loads from the v1 robot (43.7 kg) while the final CAD/v3 is 50.3 kg (brief says 51.5), the 'freeze' retrain + re-measurement + v9 re-optimization is explicitly pending (docs/74 §6, docs/90 §3), and the ankle moment reference-point correction (§8i) has not been applied to the ankle loads feeding bearing/FEA. The headline 'optimum pushed to Fusion and measured back 9/9' therefore closes the CAD loop on provisional screening inputs. A process paper whose selling point is consistency cannot present a loop that has never been closed consistently even once.
- The novelty statement 'no prior work lays out this end-to-end process' is false as written and a reviewer who knows Albers & Ottnad (Humanoids 2007/2008: controller-in-the-loop load generation for topology optimization of humanoid parts), Lola (2006-2009), Vitruvio (2020), Geonea (2026) and Disney RSS 2024 will reject on positioning alone. The defensible claim is an increment (stochastic-rollout statistics + provenance + propagation as hard constraints + stop synthesis), and it must be stated that way.

**Major weaknesses:**
- Scope is a 40-page technical report compressed into 6-8 pages: RL load campaign, design-value doctrine, Deb-lexicographic DE synthesis with 18-seed/PCA audit, closed-form IK, Fusion round trip, hard-stop architecture, CalculiX link FEA with directional envelopes, bolt detection (365 bolts), thread checks, mesh convergence, PLA substitution, mass/inertia audit, multi-agent red-team methodology. None of the individual blocks is new in method; the paper must choose one spine or it will read as an engineering report, which is exactly the category the authors are trying to escape.
- Design-value constants are conventions, not results: x1.25 on P99 for transient, 'P99 wrench x SF' for structural, 1.875 on rod force — no derivation from failure mode, duty, fatigue or the quantified uncertainty in docs/65. The doctrine's one quantitative novelty (bootstrap CIs by statistic: RMS ±3-14%, P99 ±4-18%, peak ±4-47%) is good, but it is used only to label peaks 'unreliable', not to set the factors.
- The 'rejection of raw peaks' is sound on provenance grounds (clip-saturated, out-of-distribution backward commands, collision transients) but the replacement — a 'layered stack' for falls/impacts (overload rating, clutches, control, repair design) — is a literature position (docs/56), not a designed or tested element of this robot. A reviewer will ask what happens to the 2-RSU ball joints and the gimbal stop at the first real fall; the stop energy sizing is listed as unresolved (docs/78 §14) and sim ROM occupancy of the design cap is only 33%, so the static stop-load frame rests on a premise the authors themselves flag.
- Human-gait torque coverage (5 datasets, 231 subjects, mass-scaled x1.25) is a heuristic constraint, not a requirement derived from the robot: human ankle torque scales with body mass x leg length and with a light foot/heavy trunk distribution, whereas this robot carries ~13 kg per leg with a ~0.1 kg ankle link. Kaminaga 2013 already used human-gait ankle torque plus a T-N/thermal actuator envelope inside a geometry optimization; the increment here is dataset count, which is not a method contribution. State it as a portability/robustness margin, not as a validity proxy.
- The crank-stop insufficiency argument (image of the ROM box in crank space is not a rectangle; +0.71 correlation; rectangle overshoots) is correct but elementary for any 2-DoF coupled parallel mechanism; Cassie/Digit-type ankles already put stops on the passive joint. The 'necessary and sufficient' claim for a single roll-axis gimbal stop needs a precise statement of what bounds pitch (crank stops? rod geometry?) and what 'sufficient' means under rod compliance and the 28-degree swing sensitivity noted in §15. Publishable as a short design note, not as a theorem-level contribution.
- The FEA campaign is standard aerospace-style load enveloping (unit-load superposition with sign enumeration) on CalculiX; the documented red-team reversals (77 -> 133-194 MPa at a bore, withdrawn lightweighting, 'no region yields anywhere' after mesh convergence) are honest but, presented as 'methodology', tell a reviewer that the numbers were wrong several times and there is no physical anchor to say they are right now. Keep only the wrench -> link-BC hand-off interface and one worked link in the paper.
- The multi-agent / persona / red-team research methodology (docs/42, 90 §9) is off-topic for ICRA/RA-L robotics reviewers and invites questions about who did the engineering; remove it from the contribution list and mention it in one sentence of the verification protocol.
- Single robot, single mechanism, single design point: no demonstration that the process transfers (e.g. knee four-bar, hip) or that it changes a decision a conventional peak x SF sizing would have made. The strongest internal evidence of that kind (peak x SF would over-size; P99 x1.25 would pick RS03 where peaks demand RS04) is exactly the comparison the paper should make explicit, with the consequence on mass.
- Reproducibility claim vs. availability: the value of a 'documented, reproducible process' depends on releasing the measurement scripts, demand pool (~238k samples), optimizer, closed-form IK, Fusion expressions and FEA toolchain; if the release is not ready at submission the claim collapses to 'we did it carefully'.

**What would flip the verdict:**
- A single-ankle bench rig (docs/69 already sketches one) with the two RS03s, the optimized 2-RSU geometry and a load cell or strain gauge on at least one pushrod: replay 5-10 static poses and one policy trajectory under a known external foot load, and show measured motor torque (current) and rod force agree with the closed-form IK + J^T prediction within ~15% across the ROM. This validates the load chain the whole paper rests on without needing a walking robot.
- Sim-to-sim transmission validation: re-measure ankle loads through the closed-chain 2-RSU MuJoCo model (loop ankle v21) with the actual 2x RS03 actuator limits, for the same policies/commands, and report the bias of the serial-proxy J^T statistics (RMS, P99, rod force, T-N margin) — if the design values move by less than the claimed 1.25 factor, the doctrine survives; if not, the paper has found something important either way.
- Close the loop once, consistently: freeze retrain on the final v3 model (50.3 kg, final inertias, ankle moment reference-point corrected), re-measure, re-run the v9 synthesis, and report whether the 9 optimized parameters and the FEA verdicts change. A 'the optimum is stable to within tolerance' or 'the optimum moved, here is why' result is the kind of evidence a process paper needs.
